Lesson 4 video: Reading the cutsheet and labels
Length 4:26 · transcript


[0:00] INTRODUCTION
[0:01] Every cable you install is listed on the cutsheet and labeled at both ends.
[0:08] This video shows you how to read both.
[0:11] We will cover device names, panel port codes, one cutsheet row from A to Z, and labels.

[0:22] NAMES AND LOCATIONS
[0:23] Every device has a name and a location, and the cutsheet gives both.
[0:30] A name says what a device is, from big to small: the site, the pod, the rack in that pod, and the device.
[0:40] A location says where it is, like the codes you learned in lesson two.
[0:46] Some device names you will meet: tor1 is the top of rack switch. rmu1 and rmu2 are the two TROSS units.
[0:58] And pp1 is the OCS panel on the TPU rack.

[1:07] PANEL PORT CODES
[1:08] A panel port code tells you exactly which hole on a panel to use.
[1:14] The guide shows two ways of writing the same spot.
[1:19] On the cutsheet you will see codes like r1 dash a1.
[1:24] That means row one, column a, port one.
[1:29] On the end of row panel, ports are pre-printed differently: A1 dash 1.
[1:36] The letter is the column, then the port, then after the dash, the row.
[1:42] Both point to the same hole.
[1:45] Read each piece by what it is, not by its position.
[1:50] And remember, rows count up from the bottom of the panel.
[1:55] Row one is always the lowest row.

[2:01] ONE CUTSHEET ROW
[2:02] Every cable you install has one row in the cutsheet.
[2:07] Read it from left to right.
[2:11] A and Z are the two ends of the cable.
[2:14] A is where it starts, and Z is where it finishes.
[2:20] The A side tells you where the cable starts: the location, the device name, and the port.
[2:29] The A panel is the first panel and port it lands on.
[2:34] Trunk and leg tell you which big cable is in the middle, and which leg of it.
[2:41] The Z panel is the last panel and port, and the Z side is where the cable ends.
[2:49] If a row says spare, that leg is landed and labeled, but not plugged into a device.

[2:59] LABELS
[3:00] Now labels.
[3:02] Every cable gets the same label at both ends, and it must match the cutsheet.
[3:09] Single connectors, breakout legs and copper runs get a flag label.
[3:15] It sticks out like a flag, four inches from the boot.
[3:20] The boot is the rubber cover behind the connector.
[3:26] Trunks get a wrap-around label that circles the cable, one to two inches from the boot at each end.
[3:34] All labels are machine printed.
[3:38] No handwriting.
[3:40] Line one is the A device and port.
[3:44] Line two is the Z device and port.
[3:48] Trunk labels list all the ports their legs land on.
[3:53] One to four in square brackets with a colon means ports one through four.
[3:59] Commas mean a list.
[4:02] And if a label and the cutsheet disagree: stop and ask.

[4:10] WRAP-UP
[4:11] In lesson four of the course, you will choose the right label for different cables and spot label problems in realistic situations.
