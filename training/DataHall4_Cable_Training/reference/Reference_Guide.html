DRS 01.00.00 Introduction: what a DRS is

Portal page · approved 6/9/2026 · approved by Tom Marsh, Infrastructure Deployment Manager

  1. DRS = Deployment Requirement Sheet: Fluidstack's technical standards for how installation work must be done. Requirements, not guidelines. Every on-site task has a matching DRS.
  2. Contains step-by-step requirements, specifications, approved materials, diagrams, and references to ANSI/TIA, BICSI, NEC and NEMA standards.
  3. Exists so every deployment meets the same quality standard, whoever does the work; protects equipment and lowers outage risk.
  4. Approved by Fluidstack ICT leadership. New revisions are announced through the portal. Always use the current version: check the revision date on the cover page.
  5. Get it from Fluidstack Confluence (fluidstack.atlassian.net → LVC Deployment space → DEPLOYMENT REQUIREMENTS SHEETS). Backup: your Fluidstack PM or site supervisor.
  6. On site: have the applicable DRS on hand, printed or on a device. Working from memory alone is not acceptable. No DRS: stop work and get it.

DRS 01.00.01 General Fiber Cable Dressing

Drawing + portal summary · portal title: General Cable Dressing · revised 6/9/2026 · approved by Tom Marsh

  1. Dress cables in accordance with ANSI/TIA-568, ANSI/TIA-606 and ANSI/TIA-942.
  2. Bundle with 1/2-inch hook-and-loop (Velcro). Nylon zip ties are not allowed.
  3. Bend radius: follow the manufacturer's spec; minimum 20 × diameter loaded (under tension), 10 × unloaded. See DRS 01.00.07 for fiber bend radius.
  4. Cable supports must not damage the cable: no metal staples.
  5. Support every 4-5 feet on horizontal runs (TIA-568 recommendation).
  6. Rated cable for the space: plenum OFNP / CMP in plenum spaces, riser OFNR / CMR in vertical risers, LSZH where local code requires.

Standards named: ANSI/TIA-568, ANSI/TIA-606, ANSI/TIA-942, NEC (cable ratings)

DRS 01.00.02 Front of Rack Cable Dressing

Drawing + portal summary · revised 6/9/2026 · approved by Tom Marsh

  1. Cables shall not cross devices they do not patch into. Every device must be replaceable without unpatching cables that end in another device.
  2. Cable shall exit the basket tray directly above the rack edge or the vertical cable managers (VCMs).
  3. All cables entering or leaving the basket tray must be supported by waterfalls to keep the proper bend radius.
  4. Over-the-top waterfalls must be used for network racks.
  5. Bundle cable neatly with 1/2-inch Velcro.
  6. Cables should use the waterfall next to the side of the rack they patch into.
  7. Drawing note: each waterfall lines up with the rack VCM. Tray accessories must fit trays made by Basor, WBT, Legrand or equivalent, with 2 × 4 inch (50 × 100 mm) openings and 5 mm wire.

DRS 01.00.03 Cable Dressing Basket Trays

Drawing + portal summary · portal title: Basket Tray Cable Dressing · revised 6/9/2026 · approved by Tom Marsh

  1. Bundle cables neatly in the tray with Velcro 12 to 18 inches apart.
  2. Do not secure bundles to the basket tray except going around bends, exiting the tray, or running vertically. Secure 3× at corners and 3× at exits.
  3. 1/2-inch Velcro for all cable bundling.
  4. Waterfalls centered over the rack VCMs.
  5. Cables keep the manufacturer's recommended bend radius at all times. Drawing: fiber bend radius at a tray corner shown as 20 × cable diameter.
  6. Portal summary: never allow cable to drop over a tray edge unsupported. Waterfalls keep the bend radius at every tray exit.

DRS 01.00.03.01 Cable Dressing Vertical Pathways

Drawing + portal summary · portal title: Vertical Pathway Dressing · revised 6/9/2026 · approved by Tom Marsh

  1. Bundle cables neatly with Velcro 12 to 18 inches apart, in vertical tray as well.
  2. Secure bundles to the cable tray when running vertically (horizontal bundles float).
  3. Cable in vertical tray or ladder must be secured to be self-supporting. Excessive cable weight on horizontal transitions damages the tray and the cable.
  4. Waterfalls are required at all vertical transitions to keep the cable bend radius. A missing waterfall can damage the cable.
  5. 3/4-inch Velcro may be used to secure cable to vertical tray.
  6. Drawing: all bends greater than 20 × the cable diameter. Portal: too little Velcro on vertical runs lets cable drop to the bottom under its own weight, damaging cable and tray.

DRS 01.00.01 General Fiber Cable Dressing (revised 6/9/2026)

  1. Dress cables per ANSI/TIA-568, ANSI/TIA-606 and ANSI/TIA-942.
  2. 1/2-inch hook-and-loop (Velcro) only. Nylon zip ties are not allowed.
  3. Bend radius: follow the manufacturer's spec; minimum 20× diameter loaded (under tension), 10× unloaded (no tension). See DRS 01.00.07 for fiber bend radius.
  4. Supports must not damage the cable. No metal staples.
  5. Support every 4-5 ft on horizontal runs (TIA-568 recommendation).
  6. Rated cable: plenum OFNP / CMP in plenum spaces; riser OFNR / CMR in vertical risers; LSZH where local code requires.

DRS 01.00.03 Cable Dressing Basket Trays (revised 6/9/2026)

  1. Bundle neatly in the tray with Velcro every 12-18 in.
  2. Do not secure bundles to the tray except at corners, exits and vertical runs: 3× at each corner, 3× at each exit.
  3. 1/2-inch Velcro for all cable bundling.
  4. Waterfalls centered over the rack VCMs.
  5. Keep the manufacturer's recommended bend radius at all times (drawing: 20× cable diameter at a tray corner).

Never let cable drop over a tray edge unsupported. Waterfalls keep the bend radius at every tray exit.

DRS 01.00.03.01 Cable Dressing Vertical Pathways (revised 6/9/2026)

  1. Velcro every 12-18 in, in vertical tray as well.
  2. Secure bundles to the tray when running vertically (horizontal bundles float).
  3. Vertical cable secured to be self-supporting: too much weight on horizontal transitions damages tray and cable.
  4. Waterfalls at all vertical transitions.
  5. 3/4-inch Velcro may secure cable to vertical tray (bundling stays 1/2-inch).

Drawing: all bends greater than 20× cable diameter. Too little Velcro lets cable drop to the bottom.

DRS 01.00.02 Front of Rack Cable Dressing (revised 6/9/2026)

  1. Never cross devices you do not patch into. Every device must be replaceable without unpatching another device's cables.
  2. Exit the basket tray directly above the rack edge or VCM.
  3. Waterfalls support every cable entering or leaving the tray (bend radius); each lines up with the rack VCM.
  4. Over-the-top waterfalls at network racks (guide p.39, 41: DCN-PP and spine racks).
  5. Bundle neatly with 1/2-inch Velcro.
  6. Use the waterfall on the side of the rack you patch into.

Tray accessories must fit trays made by Basor, WBT, Legrand or equivalent: 2 × 4 in (50 × 100 mm) openings, 5 mm wire.

VCM, slack and tiers (guide p.7; their DRS sheet has not been added yet)

  1. VCM: exit between the fingers closest to the device; slack downstream; nothing touches the floor.
  2. Trunk slack above the split point on top of the rack, never in the tray.
  3. Tiers from the bottom: Tier 1 OCS · Tier 2 144F + CAT6 (this site).

Guide p.7 also allows 3/4-inch Velcro on vertical tray (DRS 01.00.03.01 agrees: for securing cable to vertical tray) and recommends 15× long-term bend radius (not on any DRS so far: open item 13).

Done-when checklists

TPU rack (guide p.16)

  • 16 legs of the 144F trunk in TOR odd ports 1-31, in order (leg N in the Nth odd port).
  • Legs 17 and 18 not plugged in at the TOR. Dressed and labeled. Not touching the floor.
  • OCS cable 1 (red) in ports 1-8, cable 2 (orange) in 9-16, cable 3 (yellow) in 17-24.
  • Three spare OCS legs handled the way your site decided. Labeled.
  • 6 CAT6 cables: TOR ETH and console, RMU1 GIG E 1 and console, RMU2 GIG E 1 and console.
  • Every fiber scoped and cleaned before it went in.
  • Every cable flag-labeled at both ends, matching its cutsheet row.
  • Nothing touched on the machine trays, batteries or rectifiers.

OCS cables (guide p.23)

  • Right cable color to the right SPOCS rack: red 1, orange 2, yellow 3.

Glossary

144F trunk — 144-fiber trunk
A trunk cable with 144 fibers. It splits into 18 MPO-8 legs (18 × 8 = 144). 16 legs are used and 2 are spares. One runs from each TPU rack's TOR switch to its EOR (AIO) rack. (guide p.12, 13, 46)
192F trunk — 192-fiber trunk
A trunk with 192 fibers in 24 MPO-8 legs. Each AIO rack sends 8 of them from the back of its MPO panel, one to each DCN patch panel rack (columns A-H). (guide p.34, 37, 46)
2x2 shuffle module
A cassette at the DCN patch panel that mixes 2 MPO-8 inputs (from 2 different AIO trunks) into 2 MPO-8 outputs, so every output carries fibers from both inputs. Each module holds 2 shuffles. Large sites use P/N 760262482. If a module reads 760262479 (the 4x4), stop and ask. (guide p.38, 40)
A / Z — A end / Z end
The two ends of one cable. A is where it starts, Z is where it finishes. Every cutsheet row and every label is written A first, Z second. (guide p.6, 8)
AIO rack — All-In-One rack
The end-of-row rack. Same rack as the EOR. It holds the MPO 4RU panel (144F in front, 192F out the back), the copper panels, the management switches and the RU 39 LC panel. (guide p.25, 34, 46)
AIO-PP number — AIO patch panel number
On a DCN patch panel, the slot number a particular AIO rack's trunk uses (AIO-PP1 to AIO-PP12). One DCN-PP panel holds 12 AIO trunks. (guide p.39, 45)
ANSI/TIA — American National Standards Institute / Telecommunications Industry Association
TIA writes telecommunications standards; ANSI accredits them as American National Standards. DRS 01.00.01 says cable must be dressed per ANSI/TIA-568, ANSI/TIA-606 and ANSI/TIA-942. TIA-598 (fiber color code) is also a TIA standard. (DRS 01.00.00, 01.00.01; public (TIA))
APC — Angled Physical Contact
A fiber connector whose glass end face is polished at an 8° angle. APC connectors are usually green. Never plug an APC connector into a UPC (usually blue) connector: the cores do not line up and the end faces can be damaged. OCS legs use LC/APC. (guide p.20; public (Fluke Networks))
Basket tray — wire mesh cable tray
The overhead tray made of welded wire that cables run in above the racks. In it, bundle with 1/2-inch Velcro every 12-18 inches and secure to the tray only at corners, exits and vertical runs, 3 times at each corner and exit (DRS 01.00.03). Tray accessories such as waterfalls must fit trays made by Basor, WBT, Legrand or equivalent, with 2 × 4 inch (50 × 100 mm) openings and 5 mm wire (DRS 01.00.02). (DRS 01.00.02, 01.00.03; guide p.7)
Bend radius
How tightly a cable is allowed to curve, measured from the center of the curve out to the cable. DRS 01.00.01 rule 3: follow the manufacturer's spec, with at least 20 × the cable diameter when loaded (under tension, such as while pulling) and 10 × when unloaded. DRS 01.00.03 rule 5: keep the manufacturer's recommended bend radius at all times; its drawing marks 20 × at a tray corner, and the DRS 01.00.03.01 drawing says all bends greater than 20 ×. DRS 01.00.01 points to DRS 01.00.07 for fiber detail. The guide (p.7) adds 15 × as the long-term recommendation. (DRS 01.00.01, 01.00.03, 01.00.03.01; guide p.7)
BICSI — Building Industry Consulting Service International
A professional association for the ICT industry. It publishes design and installation standards and runs training and credentials. DRS documents reference BICSI. (DRS 01.00.00 (named); public (BICSI, ANSI))
BMS — Building Management System
A control system that monitors and manages a building's mechanical and electrical systems, such as power, cooling, water detection and fire safety. In this hall, six EOR racks are the "w/BMS" type and carry the BMS network switch (FB0 BMS) and its uplink. (guide p.25-26, 29; public (TechTarget))
BOM Builder — Bill of Materials Builder
The tool that lists every part and port for a site. The guide says exact OCS ports come from the cutsheet or BOM Builder. (guide p.21)
Boot
The rubber cover behind a connector. Label distances are measured from the boot. (guide p.8)
Breakout
A cable end where one connector splits into several smaller ones, such as one MPO plug splitting into several LC legs. (guide p.46)
CAT6 — Category 6 copper network cable
Copper network (Ethernet) cable with an RJ45 plug. Each TPU rack sends 6 CAT6 home runs to its EOR rack. (guide p.13, 46)
CDFP cable — a 400 Gb/s pluggable form factor (CD = 400 in Roman numerals)
High-speed cables used inside the TPU rack. They arrive pre-installed. You do not touch them. (guide p.4 (named only); general)
CDU — Coolant Distribution Unit (not defined in the guide)
The CDU blocks at the ends of each compute row on the hall map. In liquid-cooled data centers a CDU manages the flow, temperature and pressure of coolant to the racks. Not part of your cable work. (guide p.5 (map label only); public (Supermicro, Hot Chips 2025))
Column (panel)
A lettered position across a panel, A to L. On the MPO and LC panels each column has 2 ports. (guide p.35, 46)

The main TIA standard for building telecom cabling: how cable is installed and supported. DRS 01.00.01 names it for dressing, and its drawing says TIA-568 recommends support every 4-5 ft on horizontal runs. (DRS 01.00.01; public (TIA))
TIA-598 — TIA-598 fiber color code standard
The standard fiber color order: 1 blue, 2 orange, 3 green, 4 brown, 5 slate, 6 white, 7 red, 8 black, 9 yellow, 10 violet, 11 rose, 12 aqua. Positions 13 and up repeat the colors, so always read the number on the label. (guide p.8, 13, 47; public (FOA))
TIA-606 — Administration standard for telecommunications infrastructure
The TIA standard for administration: identifiers, labels and records, so every cable and port can be traced. DRS 01.00.01 names it for dressing. Your site's label format comes from the guide (p.8) and the cutsheet. (DRS 01.00.01; public (TIA))
TIA-942 — Telecommunications Infrastructure Standard for Data Centers
The TIA standard for the infrastructure of data centers and computer rooms, including cabling and pathways. DRS 01.00.01 names it for dressing. (DRS 01.00.01; public (TIA))
Tier
A tray level, counted from the bottom up. Tier 1: OCS cables. Tier 2: 144F trunks and, at this site, CAT6 (guide p.14 still says Tier 1 for copper: ask your lead). (guide p.7, 14, 47)
TOR switch — Top Of Rack switch
The TPU rack's door to the data network. An Arista 7060 on side A. The 144F trunk plugs into its odd ports (top row); it also sends 2 copper runs (ETH and console). (guide p.12-13, 47)
TPU — Tensor Processing Unit
A computer chip built for AI work. In this guide "TPU rack" (or just "TPU") means a rack of these AI computers. The drawings call it an Ironwood TPU rack. (guide p.11-12, 47; public (Google Cloud))
TROSS / RMU — (TROSS is not expanded in the guide)
The TPU rack's management box, one per side at RU 38. The drawings call it TROSS; the cutsheet calls it rmu1 (side A) and rmu2 (side B). Each sends 2 CAT6 runs: GIG E 1 and CONSOLE. (guide p.12, 14, 47)
Trunk
A thick multi-fiber cable that splits into legs at each end. Gets a wrap-around label 1-2 inches from the boot at each end. (guide p.6, 8, 47)
Turn-up
When a rack is powered and brought online. Final patching at the management spine rack must be done before turn-up. (guide p.30, 47)
Type B — Type B polarity
A cable that crosses send and receive, so one end's transmit fiber meets the other end's receive port. OCS cables are Type B. The cutsheet already accounts for it; you do not swap anything. (guide p.20, 47; public (SENKO))
Uplink
A connection from a switch up to a bigger switch. (guide p.30)
VCM — Vertical Cable Manager
The channel on the side of a rack. Cable leaves the tray directly above the rack edge or the VCM (DRS 01.00.02 rule 2), over a waterfall centered over the VCM (DRS 01.00.03 rule 4). Cables exit between the fingers closest to the device they patch (guide p.7). (DRS 01.00.02, 01.00.03; guide p.7, 47)
Velcro — hook-and-loop strap
The only tie allowed. Bundle cables with 1/2-inch hook-and-loop everywhere (DRS 01.00.01 rule 2; DRS 01.00.02 rule 5; DRS 01.00.03 rule 3: for all cable bundling), 12-18 inches apart in tray (DRS 01.00.03 rule 1; DRS 01.00.03.01 rule 1). 3/4-inch Velcro may be used to secure cable to vertical tray (DRS 01.00.03.01 rule 5; guide p.7). Nylon zip ties are not allowed. (DRS 01.00.01, 01.00.02, 01.00.03, 01.00.03.01; guide p.7)
Vertical pathway — vertical tray or ladder
A tray or ladder that carries cable up or down between levels. DRS 01.00.03.01: bundle with Velcro every 12-18 inches, secure the bundles to the tray so the cable is self-supporting, use waterfalls at all vertical transitions; 3/4-inch Velcro may be used to secure cable to vertical tray. Too little Velcro lets the cable slide to the bottom and damage the cable and tray. (DRS 01.00.03.01)
w/BMS — with Building Management System
An EOR rack type with extra parts: the FB0 BMS switch and a copper panel. Six in this hall: 611, 613, 1411, 1413, 2211, 2213. Their management trunks are 24F. (guide p.25, 29, 47)
Waterfall
A curved guide on the edge of the tray that supports cable where it enters or leaves the tray, so the bend stays wide. Never let cable drop over a tray edge unsupported. Waterfalls are centered over the rack VCMs (DRS 01.00.03 rule 4), on the side of the rack the cables patch into (DRS 01.00.02 rules 3, 6), and required at all vertical transitions (DRS 01.00.03.01 rule 4). Network racks use over-the-top waterfalls (DRS 01.00.02 rule 4). (DRS 01.00.02, 01.00.03, 01.00.03.01; guide p.7, 47)
Wrap-around label
A label that circles the cable. Used on trunks, 1-2 inches from the boot of each end connector, at least 1 inch wide. (guide p.8, 47)
