DRS 01.00.01  General Fiber Cable Dressing
Drawing + portal summary · portal title: General Cable Dressing · revised 6/9/2026 · approved
by Tom Marsh
1. Dress cables in accordance with ANSI/TIA-568, ANSI/TIA-606 and
ANSI/TIA-942.
2. Bundle with 1/2-inch hook-and-loop (Velcro). Nylon zip ties are not
allowed.
3. Bend radius: follow the manufacturer's spec; minimum 20 × diameter
loaded (under tension), 10 × unloaded. See DRS 01.00.07 for fiber bend
radius.
4. Cable supports must not damage the cable: no metal staples.
5. Support every 4-5 feet on horizontal runs (TIA-568 recommendation).
6. Rated cable for the space: plenum OFNP / CMP in plenum spaces, riser
OFNR / CMR in vertical risers, LSZH where local code requires.
Standards named: ANSI/TIA-568, ANSI/TIA-606, ANSI/TIA-942, NEC (cable ratings)

DRS 01.00.02  Front of Rack Cable Dressing
Drawing + portal summary · revised 6/9/2026 · approved by Tom Marsh
1. Cables shall not cross devices they do not patch into. Every device
must be replaceable without unpatching cables that end in another
device.
2. Cable shall exit the basket tray directly above the rack edge or the
vertical cable managers (VCMs).
3. All cables entering or leaving the basket tray must be supported by
waterfalls to keep the proper bend radius.
4. Over-the-top waterfalls must be used for network racks.
5. Bundle cable neatly with 1/2-inch Velcro.
6. Cables should use the waterfall next to the side of the rack they patch
into.
7. Drawing note: each waterfall lines up with the rack VCM. Tray
accessories must fit trays made by Basor, WBT, Legrand or equivalent,
with 2 × 4 inch (50 × 100 mm) openings and 5 mm wire.

DRS 01.00.03  Cable Dressing Basket Trays
Drawing + portal summary · portal title: Basket Tray Cable Dressing · revised 6/9/2026 ·
approved by Tom Marsh
1. Bundle cables neatly in the tray with Velcro 12 to 18 inches apart.
2. Do not secure bundles to the basket tray except going around bends,
exiting the tray, or running vertically. Secure 3× at corners and 3× at
exits.
3. 1/2-inch Velcro for all cable bundling.
4. Waterfalls centered over the rack VCMs.
5. Cables keep the manufacturer's recommended bend radius at all
times. Drawing: fiber bend radius at a tray corner shown as 20 × cable
diameter.
6. Portal summary: never allow cable to drop over a tray edge
unsupported. Waterfalls keep the bend radius at every tray exit.

DRS 01.00.03.01  Cable Dressing Vertical Pathways
Drawing + portal summary · portal title: Vertical Pathway Dressing · revised 6/9/2026 ·
approved by Tom Marsh
1. Bundle cables neatly with Velcro 12 to 18 inches apart, in vertical tray
as well.
2. Secure bundles to the cable tray when running vertically (horizontal
bundles float).
3. Cable in vertical tray or ladder must be secured to be self-supporting.
Excessive cable weight on horizontal transitions damages the tray and
the cable.
4. Waterfalls are required at all vertical transitions to keep the cable
bend radius. A missing waterfall can damage the cable.
5. 3/4-inch Velcro may be used to secure cable to vertical tray.
6. Drawing: all bends greater than 20 × the cable diameter. Portal: too
little Velcro on vertical runs lets cable drop to the bottom under its
own weight, damaging cable and tray.

Glossary
144F trunk — 144-fiber trunk
A trunk cable with 144 fibers. It splits into 18 MPO-8 legs (18 × 8 = 144). 16 legs are
used and 2 are spares. One runs from each TPU rack's TOR switch to its EOR (AIO)
rack. (guide p.12, 13, 46)
192F trunk — 192-fiber trunk
A trunk with 192 fibers in 24 MPO-8 legs. Each AIO rack sends 8 of them from the
back of its MPO panel, one to each DCN patch panel rack (columns A-H). (guide
p.34, 37, 46)
2×2 shuffle module
A cassette at the DCN patch panel that mixes 2 MPO-8 inputs (from 2 different AIO
trunks) into 2 MPO-8 outputs, so every output carries fibers from both inputs. Each
module holds 2 shuffles. Large sites use P/N 760262482. If a module reads
760262479 (the 4×4), stop and ask. (guide p.38, 40)
A / Z — A end / Z end
The two ends of one cable. A is where it starts, Z is where it finishes. Every
cutsheet row and every label is written A first, Z second. (guide p.6, 8)
AIO rack — All-In-One rack
The end-of-row rack. Same rack as the EOR. It holds the MPO 4RU panel (144F in
front, 192F out the back), the copper panels, the management switches and the RU
39 LC panel. (guide p.25, 34, 46)
Data Hall 4 Cable Installation · Reference Guide · page 29 of 50
AIO-PP number — AIO patch panel number
On a DCN patch panel, the slot number a particular AIO rack's trunk uses (AIO-PP1
to AIO-PP12). One DCN-PP panel holds 12 AIO trunks. (guide p.39, 45)
ANSI/TIA — American National Standards Institute / Telecommunications Industry Association
TIA writes telecommunications standards; ANSI accredits them as American
National Standards. DRS 01.00.01 says cable must be dressed per ANSI/TIA-568,
ANSI/TIA-606 and ANSI/TIA-942. TIA-598 (fiber color code) is also a TIA
standard. (DRS 01.00.00, 01.00.01; public (TIA))
APC — Angled Physical Contact
A fiber connector whose glass end face is polished at an 8° angle. APC connectors
are usually green. Never plug an APC connector into a UPC (usually blue)
connector: the cores do not line up and the end faces can be damaged. OCS legs
use LC/APC. (guide p.20; public (Fluke Networks))
Basket tray — wire mesh cable tray
The overhead tray made of welded wire that cables run in above the racks. In it,
bundle with 1/2-inch Velcro every 12-18 inches and secure to the tray only at
corners, exits and vertical runs, 3 times at each corner and exit (DRS 01.00.03).
Tray accessories such as waterfalls must fit trays made by Basor, WBT, Legrand or
equivalent, with 2 × 4 inch (50 × 100 mm) openings and 5 mm wire (DRS 01.00.02).
(DRS 01.00.02, 01.00.03; guide p.7)
Bend radius
How tightly a cable is allowed to curve, measured from the center of the curve out
to the cable. DRS 01.00.01 rule 3: follow the manufacturer's spec, with at least 20
× the cable diameter when loaded (under tension, such as while pulling) and 10 ×
when unloaded. DRS 01.00.03 rule 5: keep the manufacturer's recommended bend
radius at all times; its drawing marks 20 × at a tray corner, and the DRS
01.00.03.01 drawing says all bends greater than 20 ×. DRS 01.00.01 points to DRS
01.00.07 for fiber detail. The guide (p.7) adds 15 × as the long-term
recommendation. (DRS 01.00.01, 01.00.03, 01.00.03.01; guide p.7)
BICSI — Building Industry Consulting Service International

A professional association for the ICT industry. It publishes design and installation
standards and runs training and credentials. DRS documents reference BICSI. (DRS
01.00.00 (named); public (BICSI, ANSI))
BMS — Building Management System
A control system that monitors and manages a building's mechanical and electrical
systems, such as power, cooling, water detection and fire safety. In this hall, six
EOR racks are the "w/BMS" type and carry the BMS network switch (FB0 BMS)
and its uplink. (guide p.25-26, 29; public (TechTarget))
BOM Builder — Bill of Materials Builder
The tool that lists every part and port for a site. The guide says exact OCS ports
come from the cutsheet or BOM Builder. (guide p.21)
Boot
The rubber cover behind a connector. Label distances are measured from the boot.
(guide p.8)
Breakout
A cable end where one connector splits into several smaller ones, such as one
MPO plug splitting into several LC legs. (guide p.46)
CAT6 — Category 6 copper network cable
Copper network (Ethernet) cable with an RJ45 plug. Each TPU rack sends 6 CAT6
home runs to its EOR rack. (guide p.13, 46)
CDFP cable — a 400 Gb/s pluggable form factor (CD = 400 in Roman numerals)
High-speed cables used inside the TPU rack. They arrive pre-installed. You do not
touch them. (guide p.4 (named only); general)
CDU — Coolant Distribution Unit (not defined in the guide)
The CDU blocks at the ends of each compute row on the hall map. In liquid-cooled
data centers a CDU manages the flow, temperature and pressure of coolant to the
racks. Not part of your cable work. (guide p.5 (map label only); public (Supermicro, Hot Chips
2025))

Column (panel)
A lettered position across a panel, A to L. On the MPO and LC panels each column
has 2 ports. (guide p.35, 46)
Column pair
Two side-by-side columns on a DCN patch panel (AB, CD, EF, GH, IJ, KL) that hold
one 2×2 module. (guide p.39)
Confluence (Fluidstack portal)
The authoritative source for all current DRS documents. Log in at
fluidstack.atlassian.net, open the LVC Deployment space, then DEPLOYMENT
REQUIREMENTS SHEETS. It needs a login and a connection. If you cannot get in,
contact your Fluidstack PM or site supervisor. (DRS 01.00.00)
Console port
A port that gives a direct, text-only connection to a device for setup and recovery.
The TOR and both TROSS/RMU units each send a console run to the EOR console
panel (RU 30). (guide p.13-14; general (WTI))
Cutsheet
The spreadsheet that lists every cable and every stop it makes. Your site's
cutsheet is the final word on any port. (guide p.1, 6)
DAC cable — Direct Attach Copper
Short copper cables inside the TPU rack. Pre-installed. Not yours. (guide p.4 (named
only); general)
DCN — Data Center Network
The main data network. Its big switches are the DCN spines in network rows 1 and
2. (guide p.4, 46)
